[[FOREX AND CFDs]]
#### Basic Definition

An Islamic Forex account, also called a **Swap-Free account**, is a special type of trading account designed for Muslim traders that **removes overnight interest (swap) charges** to comply with the Islamic prohibition of Riba.

---

#### Why Were They Created?

Standard Forex accounts charge **rollover/swap fees** every night a position stays open. Since Islam strictly prohibits interest in any form, brokers created Islamic accounts to attract **Muslim traders** by eliminating this specific charge.
#### How Do Brokers Compensate for Lost Swap Revenue?

Since brokers lose their swap income on Islamic accounts, they recover it through:

---

##### Method 1 — Wider Spreads

- The broker simply increases the **spread** on Islamic accounts
- Instead of 1 pip spread, they charge 2–3 pips
- You pay more on every single trade instead of nightly

##### Method 2 — Fixed Administrative Fee

- Some brokers charge a flat **daily or weekly administrative fee**
- This fee increases the longer you hold a position
- Critics argue this is simply **Riba renamed** — the same interest by a different name

##### Method 3 — Higher Commission Per Trade

- Broker charges a **larger commission** on each trade execution
- More transparent than hidden fees but still recovers lost swap revenue

---

#### The Big Scholarly Debate — Are Islamic Accounts Actually Halal?

This is where opinions sharply divide.

---

##### 🟢 Scholars Who Accept Islamic Accounts Say:

- Removing the **explicit interest charge** satisfies the basic Riba prohibition
- The trader's **intention** matters — they are genuinely trying to avoid Riba
- Administrative fees are for a **service**, not for the time value of money
- It is a **practical solution** for Muslims living in a modern financial world
- The account structure shows **good faith effort** toward Shariah compliance

---

##### 🔴 Scholars Who Reject Islamic Accounts Say:

- Replacing swap with an **administrative fee** is just Riba with a different name
- This is **Hiyal** — a legal trick to make something haram appear halal
- The **core problems remain** — leverage, speculation, gharar, and maysir are all still present
- Removing one haram element does not purify an **entirely haram structure**
- The Prophet ﷺ warned against such tricks:

> **"The curse of Allah is upon the one who accepts Riba, the one who pays it, the one who witnesses it, and the one who records it."** _(Sahih Muslim)_

---

#### What Do Genuine Shariah-Compliant Accounts Require?

A truly Shariah-compliant Forex account according to strict Islamic finance principles should meet **all** of the following:

```
✅ No overnight swap or interest in any form
✅ No hidden administrative fees that mimic interest
✅ Immediate spot settlement (no deferred delivery)
✅ Trader must have genuine ownership of currency
✅ No excessive leverage (or no leverage at all)
✅ Trading must serve a real economic purpose
✅ Supervised and certified by a genuine Shariah board
```
#### Shariah Supervisory Board — The Trust Factor

Some brokers claim Shariah compliance by appointing a **Shariah Supervisory Board** — a panel of Islamic finance scholars who review and certify their products.

##### What to Look For:

- ✅ Are the scholars **named and qualified?**
- ✅ Is their **certification publicly available?**
- ✅ Are they independent or **paid by the broker?**
- ✅ Which **Islamic finance standard** do they follow? (AAOIFI is most respected)
- ❌ Beware of brokers who simply **claim** Shariah compliance with no real certification

#### How They Work — Simple Summary

Islamic accounts work **exactly like standard Forex accounts** with one key difference — the overnight swap fee is removed. Everything else remains the same.

> Islamic accounts remove the **one visible form of Riba** (swap) but brokers quietly recover that lost income through **wider spreads, holding fees, and higher commissions.** The money always flows back to the broker — just through a different route.

---

#### 🔑 Key Takeaway in 3 Bullet Points

- Islamic accounts are **standard accounts** with swap fees removed — nothing else fundamentally changes
- Brokers **never lose money** — they simply shift their earnings from swap fees to spreads, commissions, and holding fees
- Whether these accounts are truly halal depends on **whether holding fees are charged** and whether the trading itself involves excessive speculation — the swap removal alone is not enough for full Shariah compliance
