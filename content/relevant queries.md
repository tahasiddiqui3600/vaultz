[[FOREX AND CFDs]]

#### Category 1 — Halal or Haram

**Q1. Is Forex trading halal or haram in Islam?**

- The most fundamental question every Muslim trader asks
- Depends entirely on how the trading is conducted
- Majority of scholars lean toward haram in its standard form

**Q2. Does the trader's intention affect the ruling?**

- Trading for genuine business hedging — more likely permissible
- Trading purely to speculate and profit — more likely impermissible
- Intention matters in Islam but cannot override a prohibited structure

**Q3. Is there a difference between spot trading and futures trading?**

- Spot trading is closer to permissible — immediate exchange
- Futures involve deferred delivery which violates the hand-to-hand condition
- Most scholars consider futures clearly haram

---

#### Category 2 — Riba and Interest

**Q4. Is the overnight swap fee considered Riba?**

- Virtually all scholars agree — yes it is Riba
- It is interest charged for holding a leveraged position overnight
- This single issue alone makes standard Forex accounts haram

**Q5. Are Islamic swap-free accounts truly free of Riba?**

- Most Islamic accounts simply replace swap with administrative holding fees
- Scholars are divided — many say this is Riba with a different name
- Removing the label does not change the economic reality

**Q6. Is leverage itself a form of Riba?**

- The borrowed portion of leverage attracts swap interest
- Using borrowed money for speculation is fundamentally problematic
- Some scholars say leverage resembles Riba in structure and spirit

---

#### Category 3 — Gambling and Uncertainty

**Q7. Is speculative Forex trading considered gambling in Islam?**

- If trading purely on price prediction with no real economic purpose — yes
- Zero sum nature, high leverage and uncertain outcomes mirror gambling exactly
- Most scholars consider pure speculation as Maysir

**Q8. Does using technical analysis and strategy remove the Gharar?**

- No — analysis reduces uncertainty but does not eliminate it
- Gharar is about the nature of the contract — not the trader's skill
- Markets remain fundamentally unpredictable regardless of strategy

---

#### Category 4 — Ownership and Delivery

**Q9. Do you actually own the currency you trade in Forex?**

- No — you own a contract based on currency price movement
- No actual currency is ever delivered to you
- This violates the Islamic requirement of real ownership and possession

**Q10. Is short selling permissible in Islam?**

- No — you are selling currency you do not own
- Directly violates the hadith — do not sell what you do not have
- Considered haram by the vast majority of scholars

---

#### Category 5 — Practical Questions

**Q11. Is Forex trading permissible for hedging real business risk?**

- Most permissible use case in Islamic finance
- A business with genuine currency exposure may hedge that specific risk
- Must be spot transaction with no excessive leverage and tied to real business need

**Q12. Is Forex trading income halal — can it be used for zakat or sadaqah?**

- If the trading itself is haram — income from it is also haram
- Haram income cannot be purified through charity according to majority view
- The activity itself must stop — not just the profits given away
